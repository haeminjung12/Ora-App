
# Take-home exercise library for eating/swallowing/speech
Organized by **target muscle/region** and **functional goal**. These are commonly used in SLP/rehab contexts; the *actual* home program depends on diagnosis, swallow safety, dentition/TMJ status, and clinician exam.

---

## 1) Suprahyoid / “under-the-chin” (hyolaryngeal elevation, UES opening)
1. **CTAR — Chin Tuck Against Resistance**
   - **Targets:** suprahyoids (submental), hyolaryngeal elevation support
   - **How:** tuck chin down against ball/towel/device
   - **Notes:** used widely in dysphagia rehab; systematic reviews exist; device-based versions are common in clinics [1], [2], [3]

2. **Shaker (Head-Lift)**
   - **Targets:** suprahyoids / UES-opening mechanics
   - **How:** supine head lifts (shoulders stay down)
   - **Common protocol pattern:** sustained hold(s) + repeated lifts (often “3 holds + 30 reps” variants) [4], [5], [6]

3. **Jaw-Opening Exercise (JOE)**
   - **Targets:** suprahyoids via jaw-opening synergy
   - **How:** sustained maximal jaw opening holds (protocols vary)
   - **Use-case:** impaired hyoid elevation / UES opening patterns in dysphagia rehab [7]

4. **High-speed jaw-opening training**
   - **Targets:** suprahyoid speed/velocity components (rapid reps)
   - **Use-case:** explored for hyoid kinematics/strengthening objectives [8]

---

## 2) Tongue base retraction + pharyngeal squeeze (bolus propulsion, residue reduction)
5. **Effortful swallow**
   - **Targets:** tongue base retraction + pharyngeal pressure (task-specific swallow exercise)
   - **How:** “swallow hard” while pressing tongue firmly to palate
   - **Clinical note:** some evidence suggests it can worsen pyriform residue in some patients; clinicians screen this [9], [10], [11]

6. **Masako (tongue-hold) maneuver**
   - **Targets:** tongue–pharynx interaction (often framed as pharyngeal strengthening)
   - **How:** hold tongue forward and swallow (typically saliva)
   - **Evidence note:** used frequently; evidence base is mixed and population-specific [12], [13]

7. **Pharyngeal conditioning set (common “home sheet” bundle)**
   - **Gargle (pretend to gargle; hold)**
   - **Yawn (pretend to yawn; hold)**
   - **Tongue pull / tongue retraction (pull tongue back; hold)**
   - **Hard swallow (practice “hard swallows”)**
   - **Targets:** tongue base retraction + pharyngeal squeeze components
   - **Often delivered as:** timed holds + repetitions, clinician-filled dosing blanks [14]

---

## 3) Airway protection / laryngeal closure (reduce penetration/aspiration risk)
8. **Supraglottic swallow**
   - **Targets:** voluntary airway closure + cough clearance
   - **How:** hold breath → swallow → cough → swallow again (protocol variants exist) [15], [16]

9. **Super-supraglottic swallow**
   - **Targets:** stronger airway closure (adds “bear down”)
   - **How:** inhale → hold breath tightly + bear down → swallow → cough
   - **Safety note:** “bearing down” can raise blood pressure; commonly listed as a caution [16], [17], [18]

10. **Vocal fold adduction / glottic closure drills (voice-based, sometimes paired with swallow rehab)**
   - **Targets:** vocal fold closure strength/coordination (phonation subsystem; can support cough/airway closure goals)
   - **Examples in clinic handouts:** hard glottal attack on vowels/words; pushing/pulling phonation tasks [19], [20]

---

## 4) Respiratory muscle training (cough strength, airway clearance; sometimes swallow outcomes)
11. **EMST — Expiratory Muscle Strength Training (device-based)**
   - **Targets:** expiratory muscles (forced exhale), cough; studied for swallow safety in several neuro populations
   - **How:** blow into a spring-loaded resistance device until valve opens; progressive loading is common
   - **Evidence summaries:** systematic reviews and ASHA Evidence Map summaries report improved swallow safety metrics in some groups [21], [22], [23], [24]

---

## 5) Tongue strengthening (lingual pressure; oral bolus control/propulsion)
12. **Tongue-to-palate press (anterior)**
   - **Targets:** anterior tongue pressure
   - **How:** press tongue hard against hard palate (timed holds or reps) [25]

13. **Tongue-to-palate press (posterior)**
   - **Targets:** posterior tongue pressure (tongue base region)
   - **How:** press posterior tongue up/back against palate/target [25]

14. **Directional tongue resistance (manual tool)**
   - **Targets:** protrusion / elevation / lateralization strength and control
   - **How:** push tongue against spoon/tongue depressor while clinician/caregiver resists [26], [27]

15. **IOPI-style pressure training (biofeedback + progressive loading)**
   - **Targets:** measurable tongue pressure (kPa) with dosing/progression
   - **How:** press air-filled bulb to palate; sets/reps and %max protocols are used in studies
   - **Device basics (kPa measurement) + “home use under clinician direction” is explicitly described in manuals/vendor materials** [28], [29]

---

## 6) Lip seal + anterior containment (reduce anterior loss; improve oral containment)
16. **Lip press (isometric closure)**
   - **Targets:** orbicularis oris closure strength/endurance
   - **How:** press lips together firmly for timed holds [30]

17. **Resisted tongue-depressor lip hold**
   - **Targets:** lip closure force (center / corners)
   - **How:** hold a depressor with lips while it is gently pulled (no teeth) [30]

18. **Cheek puff hold + cheek-to-cheek air transfer**
   - **Targets:** containment endurance + coordination
   - **How:** puff and hold; move air side to side without leaking [30]

19. **OMD “closed-mouth rest posture / awareness” drills**
   - **Targets:** habitual lip closure + tongue posture awareness (myofunctional)
   - **Examples listed by ASHA:** tactile stimulation; tongue movements without jaw assistance; tongue clicks; tongue tip to alveolar ridge, etc. [31]

---

## 7) Cheek / buccinator control (pocketing control; oral containment; bolus lateralization support)
20. **Finger-in-cheek buccinator resistance**
   - **Targets:** buccinator (cheek muscle) activation/strength
   - **How:** press cheek outward against finger resistance (reps each side) [32]

21. **Tongue-to-cheek push (internal pressure against external finger)**
   - **Targets:** tongue lateral strength + cheek containment control
   - **How:** push tongue into cheek where finger presses externally; hold briefly [33]

---

## 8) Chewing / bite-force conditioning (masseter/temporalis) + chewing function
22. **Functional Chewing Training (FuCT)**
   - **Targets:** chewing function (holistic: posture + sensory/motor + food/environment adjustments)
   - **Evidence:** RCTs in children with cerebral palsy with chewing disorder; systematic review summaries exist [34], [35] 

23. **Structured gum-chewing training**
   - **Targets:** masticatory endurance/load; maximum bite force outcomes (context dependent)
   - **Evidence:** controlled trials/RCTs exist in older-adult oral function work [36]

24. **Isometric clench training (typically with a mouthpiece/appliance)**
   - **Targets:** occlusal force; masticatory muscle properties
   - **Evidence:** RCT evidence exists in older adults (use is case-dependent; TMJ/bruxism screening matters) [37]

---

## 9) Jaw ROM / mobility (trismus/TMJ; “range” more than “strength”)
25. **TheraBite jaw motion rehab program**
   - **Targets:** maximum interincisal opening (MIO) improvements in trismus pathways
   - **Evidence:** clinical record evaluations in head/neck cancer trismus; widely used [38]

26. **Tongue depressor stacking (“wooden spatula” stretching)**
   - **Targets:** progressive passive opening
   - **Evidence:** used as a comparator/control in trismus trials; common “5-5-30” style regimens appear in trial protocols [39]

---

## 10) Speech production subsystems (commonly treated alongside eating/swallow therapy)
> Speech therapy typically focuses on **task-specific speech behaviors** more than isolated strength drills. Dysarthria targets include breathing, phonation, resonance, articulation, and prosody [40].

27. **Respiratory/phonatory intensive programs (Parkinson’s examples)**
   - **Targets:** breath support + loudness/phonation efficiency
   - **Examples:** SPEAK OUT! and LSVT LOUD–style intensive voice therapy are described in the literature (not “home-only,” but home practice is usually included) [41]

28. **Clinical caution on isolated oral-motor strengthening for speech**
   - ASHA Evidence Map summaries report **insufficient evidence** for many isolated oral myofunctional exercise dosage/effects for speech/swallow outcomes, depending on population and outcome definition [42]

---

# “Most commonly prescribed” clusters (what shows up most in mainstream handouts + high-frequency pathways)
- **Suprahyoid/UES dysphagia rehab:** CTAR + Shaker [1], [4], [5]
- **Pharyngeal clearance:** Effortful swallow ± Masako; plus “pharyngeal conditioning” bundles (gargle/yawn/tongue pull/hard swallow) in many clinic sheets [9], [14]
- **Airway protection:** supraglottic and super-supraglottic swallow (with BP/strain screening) [16], [17]
- **Tongue pressure:** tongue-to-palate presses; IOPI-style biofeedback when available [26], [28] ,[29]
- **Lip seal/containment:** lip press + resisted lip hold + cheek puff/air transfer [30]
- **Trismus ROM:** TheraBite and/or tongue-depressor stacking regimens [38], [39]
- **Chewing (peds neuro):** FuCT is one of the better-studied structured chewing interventions in CP [34], [35]
